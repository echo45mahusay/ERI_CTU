# ERI_CTU
This study presents the development and evaluation of a cost-effective, decision-enabled Earthquake Recording Instrument (ERI) for Type B government buildings in the Philippines, where compliance with DPWH seismic instrumentation requirements remains low due to the prohibitive cost and complexity of commercial systems. The prototype uses two wireless sensing nodes, each composed of an ESP32 microcontroller and ADXL355 MEMS accelerometer, deployed at the ground and topmost floors of the Multipurpose Building of Cebu Technological University – Main Campus. Story displacement computed from triaxial acceleration data is evaluated by a hybrid Decision Tree and Mamdani Fuzzy Inference System, producing Safe, Warning, or Unsafe classifications with confidence levels, displayed through a web-based graphical user interface designed for non-technical users. Calibration against the Gecko SMA-HR accelerograph yielded Pearson correlation coefficients exceeding 0.97 on all axes with bias below 0.02%. Shake table validation confirmed correct classifications across all tested conditions, with displacement errors ranging from 2.57% to 6.82%. The two-node prototype costs PHP 12,373.00, a reduction of over 98% from the reference instrument, and received a usability rating of 4.40 out of 5.00. These results confirm that low-cost MEMS-based technology, when properly calibrated and paired with an embedded decision algorithm, serves as a practical alternative to commercial ERIs for post-earthquake screening in resource-constrained Type B government buildings.
